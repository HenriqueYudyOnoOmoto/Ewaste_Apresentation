Overview

Metals in Your Phone

About one quarter of your phone's weight is metal (estimate):

About 40 to 50 grams of metal About 22% to 28% of the phone's weight (estimate).
Dozens of chemical elements Many elements of the periodic table, in the palm of your hand.
What They Do

The Function of Each Metal

The practical role of each metal in everyday phone operation:

Copper (~16 g) • Electricity Carries electricity and signals through the whole phone.
Gold (14 to 34 mg) • Safe Contacts Does not rust, so the connections keep working well.
Palladium (~15 mg) • Small Parts Used in tiny parts that hold electricity.

☣️ Slide 3 • The Three Dangers

Chemical threats that happen when e-waste is discarded in common trash or open dumps.

🧪 Chemical Contamination

Dangerous Materials

Lead, mercury, and cadmium can be found in old electronics.

It absorved by the body over time and can cause serious health problems.
💧 Water Contamination

Water Pollution

The rain fall over e-waste and carries toxic chemicals into the soil.

This dark liquid can reach groundwater.
🔥 Atmospheric Pollution

Burning material Makes Dangerous Smoke

Burning wires outside to get copper makes smoke with very dangerous chemicals.

Your can develop the risk of cancer. Toxic smoke moves with the wind and harms nearby people.

♻️ Slide 6 • The Concept of WEEE & REEE

Any unused device with a plug, cord, or battery, and the 4-line classification.

Everyday Rule

Technological Waste

Any device with a plug, cord, battery, or circuit, we don't use anymore.

From earbuds to refrigerators, they mix plastics, toxic substances, and precious metals.

Simple rule: Do you not use it anymore, and does it have a plug, cord, battery, or circuit? Then it is e-waste.
Definition

When a Device Stops Working

Electrical and electronic devices that are old, broken, or do not work anymore.

WEEE Waste Electrical & Electronic Equipment International Standard
REEE Resíduos Eletroeletrônicos Electronic waste in Portuguese
Classification in 4 Lines
Green Line

IT & Mobile: Computers, laptops, tablets, and smartphones.

Brown Line

Audio & Video: TVs, monitors, soundbars, and audio gear.

White Line

Home Appliances: Refrigerators, stoves, and washing machines.

Blue Line

Portables & Tools: Small electric appliances and power tools.

🔋 Slide 7 • Hope: Science is Taking Action

New research develop new way to recycling.

🚗 Clean Mobility

Electric Vehicle Batteries

In lab tests, recycling recovers aluminum and lithium. It saves like 1 ton of rock per battery.

Recovers aluminum and lithium. Nickel and cobalt are not recovered by this method.
💻 Microelectronics

Processor Gold Recovery

In lab tests, some methods recover 70% to 99% of the gold from processors.

Lab tests: 70% to 99% of the gold. Silicon from finished chips is not recovered today.
🥇 Olympic Medals

Tokyo: 5,000 Medals

About 78,000 tons of donated electronics were used to make about 5,000 Olympic medals.

Metal recovered: about 30 kg of gold, 4 tons of silver, and 2.7 tons of bronze.

Much of this metal is still sitting in people's drawers.

✅ Slide 8 • What to Do: The Practical Guide

Simple actions in two steps: while you use the device, and when you throw it away.

Phase 1

In Use & Maintenance

🛠️ Care before replacing Update the software and change the battery before buying a new phone.
🛒 Buy used Buy used phones when you can. Take old car batteries back to the shop when you buy a new one.
Phase 2

In Conscious Disposal

🗄️ Don't keep it in a drawer Give away or recycle what you don't use. Old batteries in drawers can become dangerous.
📍 Take it to a collection point Go to an official collection point (PEV). Check the hours and what they accept before you go.
🌍 UN estimate: If the world recycled all its e-waste 900 million tons of rock saved (estimate)

If all e-waste from 2022 were recycled, about 900 million tons of rock might not need to be mined.
